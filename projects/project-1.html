First author · ASSETS 2025

Small Devices, Large Changes: Investigating the Impact of a University-Government 3D-Printed Assistive Technology Program on Residents with Disabilities

How a public agency and university makerspace built the infrastructure to distribute free, practical 3D-printed assistive devices—and how recipients experienced the program.

Publication ↗
Timeline
Sep 2023–Mar 2025
Participants
10 program users
Methods
Interviews, program records, thematic analysis
Program reach
473 devices · 302 recipients

Challenge

Access depended on more than making a device.

Low-cost assistive devices can address everyday needs that commercial products and insurance do not cover. Scaling them required coordinated production, fulfillment, quality control, outreach, and trust across university and government partners.

My roleLed the follow-up study as first author, including participant interviews, transcript preparation, inductive coding, thematic analysis, synthesis, and writing.

Research approach

Following the program from delivery to lived experience

RequestResidents selected devices through a public form.
ProduceUniversity and state teams printed, tracked, and shipped devices.
LearnTen recipients joined 18–34 minute interviews in Nov 2024.

Key insights

01

Small devices supported everyday independence.

Recipients used simple tools such as jar openers, bag carriers, and page holders in daily routines, work, and community life.

02

Trust turned recipients into advocates.

Positive experiences encouraged people to recommend the program and help others request devices independently.

03

Visibility remained a major access barrier.

People struggled to relocate the request form or misunderstood eligibility, showing that outreach is part of service design.

Outcome

Research translated into infrastructure guidance.

The study identifies practical needs for shared tracking, dedicated staffing, quality assurance, multimodal outreach, and community-advocate roles. During the documented period, the university produced 210 devices to extend state capacity; follow-up survey data showed 90.51% high satisfaction.
